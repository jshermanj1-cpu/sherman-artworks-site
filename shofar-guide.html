How to Choose a Shofar

Kudu or ram's horn, which size, decorated or custom engraved, and how to care for it.

We decorate kudu and ram's horn shofars by hand in 925 silver in our family studio in Israel. This is what we tell people who ask us which one to buy.

The short answer

  • A ram's horn is the traditional shofar for Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur: curved, compact and 30 to 65 cm long.
  • A kudu horn is much longer, up to 129 cm, with a deep spiral and a lower, more resonant sound.
  • For a first shofar at home or in synagogue, a ram's horn in the M or L size is a practical choice.
  • Our shofars are decorated by hand in 925 silver, with a choice of designs or your own engraved symbol and inscription.
  • Keep the horn dry and away from heat and sun, and never use silver polish on the plating.

Kudu or ram's horn?

The ram's horn is the classic shofar of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and many communities prefer it because it recalls the ram that Abraham offered in place of Isaac. It is curved and compact, easy to hold and to carry.

The kudu horn is the long, spiralled shofar used in Yemenite communities. It gives a deeper, fuller sound and makes a striking piece on display, but it is heavier and needs more room.

If the shofar will be blown in synagogue, ask your rabbi which horn your community uses.

Decorated or custom engraved?

Our decorated shofars carry a 925 silver design: Jerusalem, the lions of Jerusalem, the Star of David, the Menorah, the Hoshen stones and more.

A custom shofar is engraved with a symbol of your choice and an optional inscription in Hebrew or English, such as a name, a date or a dedication. It makes a meaningful bar mitzvah, wedding or community gift.

On a ram's horn the Jerusalem symbol is large, so an inscription goes on the other side of the horn. On ram's horns the 925 silver plating may come in one continuous piece or in two parts, depending on the shape of the horn, and you can note a preference when you order.

How to blow a shofar

Place the mouthpiece at the corner of your mouth rather than the centre, keep your lips tight, and blow a short buzzing burst of air through them. Start gently: the sound comes from the vibration of your lips, not from blowing hard.

On Rosh Hashanah the shofar sounds three calls: tekiah, one long blast; shevarim, three medium blasts; and teruah, nine or more short blasts.

A new shofar takes some practice, and every horn sounds a little different. Practise in short sessions so your lips do not tire.

Caring for your shofar

Keep the horn dry and at room temperature, away from direct sun and heat sources. Wipe the 925 silver decoration with a soft dry cloth. Do not use silver polish or liquid cleaners on the plating. Rinse the mouthpiece with a little warm water after blowing and dry it fully before storing.

Natural horn can have a smell, especially when it is new or after it gets damp. Airing it and keeping it dry helps.

Shofar Questions

Which is better, a kudu or a ram's horn shofar?

Neither is better; they suit different needs. A ram's horn is the traditional choice for Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur and is compact and easy to carry. A kudu horn is longer, with a deeper sound, and is used in Yemenite communities.

What size shofar should I buy?

For a first shofar at home or in synagogue, a ram's horn in the M (40-44 cm) or L (45-49 cm) size is practical. Choose a longer kudu horn for a deeper sound or for display.

Can a shofar be engraved with a name?

Yes. Our custom ram's horn and kudu shofars carry an engraved symbol of your choice and an optional inscription in Hebrew or English.

How do I care for a 925 silver-plated shofar?

Keep it dry and away from heat and direct sun, wipe the plating with a soft dry cloth, and never use silver polish or liquid cleaners on it. Rinse the mouthpiece with a little warm water after blowing and dry it fully.

Is it hard to blow a shofar?

It takes a little practice. Place the mouthpiece at the corner of your mouth, keep your lips tight and buzz them gently; the sound comes from the vibration, not from blowing hard.

How long does a shofar take to arrive?

Decorated shofars are made to order, with up to 14 business days in the studio before dispatch. Custom engraved shofars usually take 2-4 weeks from the day we agree on the design. Shipping is ₪35 within Israel and $45 worldwide.
