How to Choose a Kiddush Cup

How much it should hold, glass or ceramic, 925 silver-plated or gold-plated, and the right cup for a gift.

We make Kiddush cups by hand in our family studio in Israel. These are the questions people ask us before they buy, answered by the people who make the cups.

The short answer

  • Choose a cup that holds at least a revi'it, the minimum measure for Kiddush. Ask your rabbi which measure your family follows.
  • Glass brings colour and light to the table. Ceramic carries a decorated design.
  • A 925 silver-plated rim suits a classic table, a gold-plated rim a warmer one. Both are washed by hand.
  • Tall cups, about 21 cm, stand out on the Shabbat table. Shorter cups, about 9.5 cm, are easier to hold and to store.
  • For a gift, a cup with its matching plate makes a complete set.

How much should a Kiddush cup hold?

Kiddush is said over a cup that holds at least a revi'it of wine or grape juice. Opinions differ on how much a revi'it is, and two measures are the most widely followed:

OpinionMillilitresFluid ounces
Rabbi Chaim Na'ehabout 86 mlabout 2.9 fl oz
Chazon Ishabout 150 mlabout 5.1 fl oz

Ask your rabbi which measure your family follows. Custom is to use a whole cup, without chips or cracks, filled to the brim.

Glass or ceramic?

Our glass cups are shaped and coloured by hand, so no two are exactly alike. Light passes through the glass, so the colour looks different by candlelight than by day. They come in blue, blue-green, red, orange, white, black and multicoloured glass.

One tall glass design carries the blessing over wine, Bore Pri HaGafen, and makes an especially meaningful gift.

Our ceramic cup is about 9.5 cm tall, decorated with a Menorah and Aramaic lettering, and coated with FDA-approved crystal epoxy.

See all glass and ceramic Kiddush cups

925 silver-plated or gold-plated?

Every cup has a plated rim. 925 silver plating gives a bright, classic finish that suits a white tablecloth and silver candlesticks. Gold plating is warmer and looks especially rich on black, blue and red glass.

Plating is a thin layer, so treat it gently. Wash the cup by hand, dry the rim with a soft cloth and keep it out of the dishwasher. Avoid liquid silver polish, which wears the plating.

Tall or low?

Our tall cups are about 21 cm (8.3 in) high and give the Kiddush cup pride of place on the table. Our shorter cups are about 9.5 cm (3.7 in), easier to hold and to store, and a good choice for a second cup or for a child's own Kiddush.

Do you need a plate?

A plate under the cup catches the wine that spills over when the cup is filled to the brim, and protects the tablecloth. Our glass plates are 15 cm across and made to match the cups in colour and finish.

A matching plate costs less when you buy it together with a cup.

Choosing a Kiddush cup as a gift

  • Bar or bat mitzvah: a tall cup in their favourite colour, for their own table one day.
  • Wedding: a cup with its matching plate, or two cups for the couple.
  • New home or a Shabbat host: a cup in a colour that suits their table.
  • Parents and grandparents: the tall cup with the Bore Pri HaGafen blessing.

Questions

Kiddush Cup Questions

What is the best material for a Kiddush cup?

Any whole cup that holds a revi'it can be used for Kiddush, whether glass, ceramic, silver or another material. Choose by how you will use it: glass for colour and light, ceramic for a decorated design, and a 925 silver-plated or gold-plated rim for a festive finish.

How many ml should a Kiddush cup hold?

At least a revi'it. The most common measures are about 86 ml (Rabbi Chaim Na'eh) and about 150 ml (Chazon Ish). Ask your rabbi which you follow.

Can a Kiddush cup go in the dishwasher?

Ours cannot. Wash it by hand and dry the plated rim with a soft cloth, without liquid silver polish.

Is a 925 silver-plated Kiddush cup as good as solid silver?

For Kiddush, yes: the material does not change the blessing. 925 silver plating gives the look of silver at a lower price, and it needs gentler cleaning than solid silver.

How long does a handmade Kiddush cup take to arrive?

Each cup is made to order. Allow up to 14 business days in the studio, then delivery. Shipping is ₪35 within Israel and $45 worldwide. Before a holiday, order a few weeks early.

Can I collect a Kiddush cup in person?

Yes. In Israel you can choose collection from our gallery in Karnei Shomron at checkout, with no shipping charge.
